    Concepts

    Frenet frame and reference line

    ReferenceLine is an arc-length parameterized cubic spline through a polyline (any number of points; it extends straight past both ends). It converts full kinematic states exactly between Cartesian (x, y, yaw, v, a, kappa) and time-domain Frenet (s, ṡ, s̈, d, ḋ, d̈); heading and curvature of planned trajectories come from the analytic velocity and acceleration vectors.

    Map centerlines are often noisy: digitized or recorded lanes carry vertices a few centimetres off the line, sometimes nearly on top of each other. An interpolating spline turns that into curvature spikes, which cap the speed (through the curvature limit) and jolt the Cartesian acceleration of planned trajectories. With a map, the lane chain is therefore first smoothed (smoothPolyline): resampled every metre, fitted with a second-difference penalty that removes wiggles shorter than route.smoothing_length (20 m), and reweighted until no point moves more than route.smoothing_tolerance (0.1 m), so real curves keep their shape. On the NGSIM US-101 lanes this lowers the 95th-percentile curvature from 0.017 to 0.002 1/m. Paths given with setReferencePath are used as they are.

    Corridor and lanes

    A Corridor is a set of lane bands [right(s), left(s)] expressed in the reference frame. With a map, buildRouteCorridor follows the ego's lane chain along the route and adds adjacent lanes using their real boundaries, so curved and merging lanes keep their geometry. Each band carries:

    • change_allowed: whether the ego may enter it (line markings or the routing graph; a route through the neighbour implies the change is legal);
    • extra_cost and urgency: the route's claim on the lane (below);
    • speed_limit.

    The union of allowed lanes is the road boundary used by the off-road check. Active stop lines (red lights) become virtual static obstacles.

    Route urgency. The route is a list of lane ids in driving order that ends in the destination lane; setMap optionally takes a goal point in that lane. Along each band the planner looks for the points where the band cannot go on as it is, also beyond the reference line:

    • where a route lane leaves the route: the route's lane changes from there are due, until it continues lengthwise;
    • where a band off the route ends or can no longer reach the route over permitted changes: the changes back to it are due;
    • at the end of a dead-end road, and at the goal: the changes into the lane the route continues in, or into the destination lane.

    For n changes due in a distance D, with U = max(urgency_distance, urgency_time · v) (80 m, 10 s), the urgency is u = clamp(1 − D / (n·U), 0, 1) and the cost w_route_urgency · n · u / (1 − u) (w = 10, capped at 10 w n): nothing while the changes fit comfortably, w · n with half a ramp per change left, then quickly more. The more changes are needed, the earlier they start, and changes further along the route wait for their own deadlines. A band takes its most urgent deadline. Lanes off the route cost another w, so leaving the route to pass slower traffic has to be worth it.

    Sampling and cost

    End states form a grid over lateral offset (num_width samples per allowed lane) × target speed × horizon. Lateral motion is a quintic and longitudinal motion a quartic polynomial. The cost follows Werling et al.: horizon, terminal speed error, terminal offset from the lane center and lane-change/urgency costs (all known from the end state), plus mean squared acceleration and jerk. The end-state part is a lower bound of the full cost, which FISS uses as its heuristic.

    Keeping distance

    Obstacles are projected into the Frenet frame once per cycle (extent [s_min, s_max] × [d_min, d_max] and along-track speed on a 0.5 s grid), so the terms below are cheap interval tests for every candidate. Gap and time-to-collision rules apply to obstacles moving along the reference line (within 45°) or standing still; crossing and oncoming traffic is left to the collision check.

    Car following. Per lane, the vehicle ahead sets a following speed v_lead + (gap − (desired_gap + desired_time_headway · v_lead)) / follow_time. Candidates ending in that lane are scored against it instead of the desired speed, so the speed term no longer pulls the ego into the vehicle ahead. The lane's deficit to the desired speed is charged as a lane cost, so a free neighbouring lane stays attractive and the ego overtakes when that pays off. This cost fades with the urgency of the ego lane (1 − u): a faster lane that the route requires leaving does not hold back the change into a slower one.

    Following and stopping (Werling et al., sampling.follow_lead). Besides the grid, every cycle tries candidates in the ego lane that end behind the vehicle ahead at its speed after T = 1 s, 2 s, … max_t, at the desired gap (desired_gap + desired_time_headway · v) or just outside the safety envelope (0.5 m beyond its gap), and continue at that speed. Behind a stopped vehicle or an active stop line they stop at that gap instead, over the time a constant deceleration would take (never shorter than the deceleration limit allows); from rest they stay or creep up to the gap. Their position-controlled ends and short durations give firm braking and exact stops the grid's speed-controlled samples (3–5 s) cannot. The cheapest valid candidate of grid and follower wins, in all four planners alike.

    Proximity cost (CostTerms.proximity, per-sample means like the comfort terms):

    Term Penalizes Default
    w_headway gap ahead below desired_gap + desired_time_headway · v 10, 3 m + 1.5 s
    w_ttc time to collision with the vehicle ahead below desired_ttc 5, 4 s
    w_clearance lateral gap to an obstacle alongside below desired_clearance 5, 1 m

    Each term is (1 − actual / desired)² when positive and 0 otherwise; obstacles behind the ego are ignored. Because it is computed when a candidate is generated and is never negative, the lazy planners still validate in exact cost order.

    Safety envelope (SafetyConfig, a hard "virtual bumper"): a candidate is rejected (safety_distance) if its gap to an obstacle ahead in its path falls below min_gap + min_time_headway · v (2 m + 0.3 s) or its time to collision below min_ttc (1.5 s). An obstacle already in the path, or one that cuts into the ego's lane, is checked only from reaction_time (0.5 s) after it appears and may not be approached closer than it appeared, so after a close cut-in the braking candidates stay feasible. Lane changes towards an obstacle get no such grace; samples that move laterally away from an obstacle (pulling out to overtake) are held to its time to collision but not to the gap floor, so an overtake that cannot be completed still leaves room to brake. Standing still never breaches the gap floor (it closes no gap), so a vehicle that came to rest too close may stay. The optional QP smoother respects the same envelope.

    Turning across traffic. The gap and TTC rules cover traffic heading within 45° of the ego. Towards crossing and oncoming vehicles the envelope is a post-encroachment time instead: a candidate may not occupy space such a vehicle occupies within crossing_time_gap (0.75 s) before or after it, so an unprotected left turn either waits for a gap or goes clearly ahead, instead of squeezing past within the collision margin. Like the other envelope rules it gives way when no candidate satisfies it (see the fallbacks below), so the rule alone never forces a stop halfway through a turn.

    Prediction

    Obstacles carry their own predicted states, so any predictor can be plugged in. predictLaneFollowing moves an obstacle along its lane of a RoadMap at constant speed (straightest successor at forks), decaying its lateral offset to the centerline; it falls back to constant velocity off the map, when the obstacle is not heading along its lane, or when it is nearly stopped. completePredictions gives every moving obstacle that arrives with only its current state (as tracked objects from perception usually do) such a prediction; the ROS nodes apply it according to prediction.mode.

    The four planners

    Generates Validates
    FOP all candidates all candidates (optionally in parallel)
    FOP+ all candidates in cost order until one passes
    FISS lazily, along a discrete gradient descent over the grid, guided by the previous cycle's solution the cheapest generated candidate; repeat
    FISS+ lazily, by best-first neighbour exploration; then continuous refinement of the end state with a decaying step the cheapest generated candidate; refined candidates if they beat it

    All four return the same kind of Trajectory and share every check.

    Validation

    A candidate is accepted if it is finite and within speed, acceleration, curvature and lateral-acceleration limits, stays inside the road bounds (heading-aware footprint), respects the safety envelope, and its inflated box does not overlap any obstacle's predicted box at the same time (separating-axis test with a circle pre-check). A start state already at or beyond a limit (e.g. stitched onto a full stop) may not get worse rather than failing outright. Below 1 m/s, where curvature is not checked, a candidate may not move more sideways than along the lane (more than 45° off it): a car cannot crab. Samples slower than 0.05 m/s keep the heading they came with, since the direction of so small a velocity means nothing.

    With collision.ignore_rear_in_lane (on in the CARLA runner and the ROS configs), lane-keeping candidates ignore obstacles that start behind the ego in its own lane: with traffic that reacts, the follower keeps its distance. Lane changes still check them.

    Every rejection is counted per reason (PlanStats.rejections), and the obstacle that rejected the cheapest candidate is reported as PlanStats.blocking_id (with blocking_t), together with the gap, time headway and time to collision of the vehicle ahead (PlanStats.lead).

    Smoothing (optional)

    The chosen trajectory defines a space–time corridor: at every time, the lateral interval free of road edges and of obstacles on the side it passes them, and the longitudinal interval free of obstacles sharing its lateral band. Two piecewise-jerk QPs (lateral, then longitudinal; OSQP) smooth the trajectory inside that corridor while keeping the sampled speed profile's timing. The result is used only if it passes the same validation.

    Stitching and fail-safes

    LocalPlanner plans from the previous trajectory at t + latency while the vehicle tracks it within tolerance (otherwise from the measured state). If no candidate is valid, it falls back in this order:

    1. keep the rest of the previous plan while it is still collision free (fallback);
    2. if the safety envelope rejected candidates, re-plan with the envelope as a cost instead of a check, with the full collision check: a close but collision-free plan is safer than hard braking in traffic, and the one that breaches the envelope least wins (degraded). The breach is the worst relative shortfall of the gap below its floor or of the time to collision below min_ttc, charged at envelope_retry_weight (1000) per unit, so after a close cut-in the retry brakes firmly instead of comfortably;
    3. re-plan the same way with collisions checked only over the first degraded_horizon seconds (2.5 s), since long-horizon predictions are the least reliable (degraded);
    4. brake at the limit in its lane (emergency).

    After an emergency stop the next plan starts from the measured state, with its initial deceleration capped at recovery_decel (2 m/s²): stitching onto the stop profile would carry full braking into every new plan and bring the vehicle to a standstill even once the way is clear. Likewise, below 1 m/s plans start with at most the deceleration 3 v / max_t, with which a polynomial stop within the horizon does not back up: a vehicle coming to rest does not keep decelerating once it stands.

    Control

    MpcController is a linear time-varying MPC on kinematic-bicycle error dynamics (lateral, heading, along-track and speed errors) with steering angle/rate and acceleration limits. StanleyPidController is the simple baseline. Both track the time-parameterized trajectory.
